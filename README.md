# 18.01SC Single Variable Calculus

My notes and problem sets for [MIT OCW 18.01SC](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/), taught by Prof. David Jerison. Working through it on my own.

I'm not putting solutions up here. OCW already ships answer keys, and reading them teaches you nothing. What I wanted instead was a record of how my thinking changed over time:

- `notes/` concepts written in my own words
- `ps/` problem sets, with the wrong attempts left in
- [error-log.md](error-log.md) every mistake I made and why
- [bridges.md](bridges.md) running notes toward 18.02

[error-log.md](error-log.md) is the part that matters most. The same mistake showing up again three weeks later is an honest measure of whether I'm actually improving, and I'd rather see that than hide it.

MIT budgets about 150 hours for this course. We'll see what I actually spend.

## How I study

Watch the lecture, write up the notes from a closed notebook, then do the recitation problems *before* watching the recitation video. Problem set by hand, timed, no notes open. Log what I got wrong, commit.

Two things I keep having to remind myself of. Never open the solution before I've genuinely attempted the problem. And log the mistakes even when I somehow landed on the right answer anyway, because right answer wrong method is the one that sneaks past.
