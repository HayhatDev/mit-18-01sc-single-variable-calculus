# mit 18.01sc single variable calculus

im working through [mit opencourseware 18.01sc](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/) on my own. its the one with prof jerison.

im not putting solutions up here. ocw already has the answer keys and reading them doesnt teach you anything. what i actually wanted was a record of how my thinking changed over time, so:

- `notes/` stuff written in my own words
- `ps/` problem sets, wrong answers and everything
- [error-log.md](error-log.md) every mistake i made and why
- [bridges.md](bridges.md) running notes toward 18.02

the error log is the part that matters most. the same mistake showing up again three weeks later is an honest way to see whether im actually improving, and i would rather show that than hide it.

mit says about 150 hours for this one. well see what i actually spend.

## how i study

watch the lecture, write the notes from a closed notebook, then do the recitation problems before watching the recitation video. problem set by hand, timed, no notes open. log what i got wrong, commit.

two things i keep having to remind myself of. never open the solution before ive genuinely tried the problem. and log the mistakes even when i landed on the right answer anyway, because right answer wrong method is the one that sneaks past.
