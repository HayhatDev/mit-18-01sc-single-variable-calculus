## What this repo is (and isn't)

This is not a solutions archive. Solutions archives already exist — the OCW
problem sets come with answer keys, and reading them teaches almost nothing.

This is a record of **how my understanding changed over time**:

- `notes/` — concepts rewritten in my own words, in my own structure
- `ps/` — problem sets worked by hand, including the wrong attempts
- [`error-log.md`](error-log.md) — every first-attempt mistake, diagnosed
- [`bridges.md`](bridges.md) — 1D concept → its 18.02 generalization

The commit history is the real artifact. It is a timestamped, tamper-evident
record of consistent effort. Don't rewrite it, don't squash it.

MIT budgets about **150 hours** for this course. Whatever pace I finish at,
the log will show the real number.

## The study loop

Run this per session. It's the whole method.

1. **Watch** the lecture video segment.
2. **Write notes from a closed notebook.** Definition → picture → *why it
   holds* → one worked example → one non-example (a near-miss case that breaks
   the claim). Don't copy the textbook's framing; if I can only restate
   Jerison's wording, I haven't understood it yet.
3. **Do the recitation problems *before* watching the recitation video.** The
   recitations are the most valuable material in this course and nearly
   everyone skips them. Watching first destroys the point.
4. **Problem set, by hand, timed, no notes open.**
5. **Log every error** in `error-log.md` with a one-line root cause.
6. Commit. Even if it's one problem.

### Two rules that keep this honest

- **Never read the solution before attempting, and log failures even when the
  final answer came out right.** Right answer / wrong method is the most
  expensive kind of mistake, and the easiest to hide.
- **Re-read `error-log.md` weekly** and mark entries fixed. A repeated entry
  means the root cause was never actually addressed, and it's the fastest
  signal for what to study next.
